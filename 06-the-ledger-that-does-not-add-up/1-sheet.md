# The Ledger That Does Not Add Up

Objective: after this, you can state a conservation invariant over a function, write a recursive operator (SumOver) that TLC runs, explain why a two-write transfer across two systems breaks the sum under a crash whichever order the writes take (debit first: money vanishes; credit first: money shows twice), say why distributed atomicity (two-phase commit) is costly, and design and check a transactional outbox: the debit and an outbox row in one local transaction, a relay that delivers at least once, and a credit keyed by loan id, checked for Conserved in every state and AllSettled under weak fairness.

Bug: month-end reconciliation finds the treasury ledger debited €50,000 for loan 4471 and the bakery's loan account never credited. The worker writes the debit (payout database) and the credit (the loans team's service) as two writes; a deploy landed between them. The restarted worker retries from the top and debits twice. The proposed swap (credit first) instead credits the loan twice.
Fix: act 2 of the lab: the outbox written in the same transaction as the debit, plus the loan ledger's credit keyed by loan id; Conserved holds and every loan settles with 2 loans (37 states) and 3 loans (181 states).

Key words: treasury ledger (not "fund ledger", "bank"; the variable is fund), loan ledger (not "loan service" as a term), loan account (one entry of credit), pot (the €200,000; Pot in the spec), write (a debit or a credit), debit, credit, conservation invariant (not "balance invariant", "sum invariant"), at rest (not "idle", "quiescent", "settled"), in flight (not "in transit", "pending" as a term), outbox (not "queue", "event table"), relay (not "publisher", "forwarder", "dispatcher"), at least once (not "retry semantics"), keyed credit / credit keyed by loan id (not "dedupe", "upsert" as a term), settle / AllSettled (not "complete"), two-phase commit (not "XA", "distributed transaction" after its definition), Crash (the action; "deploy" for the real event), CrashRelay, job (one loan's transfer; pc is per loan in this lesson), worker, loan, state, step, behavior, invariant, counterexample, TLC.

Terms (new in this lesson; earlier terms reused unchanged):
- conservation invariant: an invariant saying a sum over every place a quantity can be never changes; here the treasury plus every loan account plus what the outbox owes equals the pot.
- recursive operator: an operator whose definition uses itself, declared first with RECURSIVE; SumOver(S, f) sums f over S by taking one element out at a time.
- CHOOSE: `CHOOSE y \in S : P` is some y in S satisfying P, always the same one for the same S and P.
- at rest: no job is between its two writes (every pc is todo or done).
- in flight: money that has left one ledger and not yet reached the other; it must be recorded durably to be counted after a crash.
- two-phase commit: a coordinator asks every participant to prepare (promise and hold locks), then tells all to commit; a coordinator crash between the phases leaves prepared participants blocked.
- transactional outbox: a table in the same database as the debit; the debit and a row naming the owed credit commit in one local transaction, and a relay later delivers the row.
- relay: the process that reads outbox rows, asks the loan ledger to credit them, and deletes delivered rows; after a crash it redelivers, so delivery is at least once.
- at least once: every row is delivered one or more times, never zero; combined with a keyed credit the effect is exactly once.

Misconception: "debit first, or credit first, is the safe order." Why wrong: TLC breaks both in 2 steps out of 27 states; debit first is only ever short (−€50,000, −€30,000), credit first only ever over (+€30,000, +€50,000).
Second misconception: "an idempotency key fixes the dual write." Why wrong: the key makes a repeated write harmless, not the gap; credit first with a key still fails at rest in 4 of 23 states; debit first with a key is unchanged (27 states, 8 bad) because the repeated write is the debit.
Third misconception: "the outbox is a message queue" / "exactly-once delivery". Why wrong: the outbox's value is the shared transaction with the debit (two transactions: TLC breaks it in 2 steps); delivery is at least once and the keyed credit makes the effect once.

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner):
1. Not a bug: between two writes to two systems the money is in neither ledger, so the exact sum cannot hold mid-transfer in any two-write design (TLC `DebitFirstSum.cfg`: Conserved fails after Debit(4471) with no crash; ConservedAtRest passes in 9 states). The debit-first desk needs "at rest" because its in-flight money is recorded only in a worker's memory (pc = debited), which a crash erases (ConservedMem fails in 2 steps). The outbox needs no guard: Pay debits and records the owed amount in one step, and every term of Conserved (fund, InFlight from the outbox table, Credited) lives in a durable table, so it holds in all 37 states. Common wrong answer: "the outbox is never mid-transfer"; it is (row delivered, not yet deleted), but the InFlight term covers it.
2. InFlight = €30,000 (only 4472: 4471's credit has landed, credit[4471] ≠ 0); Credited = €50,000; total 120,000 + 30,000 + 50,000 = 200,000. 4471 is left out because its money is already counted in Credited; counting a delivered-but-not-deleted row would count it twice. The state is reachable: Pay(4471), Pay(4472), Deliver(4471). Without the key, after CrashRelay and a second Deliver(4471): credit[4471] = 100,000, InFlight still 30,000, total 120,000 + 30,000 + 100,000 = 250,000 (Conserved violated, as in TLC's `OutboxNoKey.cfg` run).
3. Delete-first turns at least once into at most once. TLC (`OutboxAckFirst.tla`: Take deletes the row into the relay's memory, then Deliver credits): ConservedMem (treasury + outbox owed + relay memory + credited) violated in 3 steps, Pay(4471), Take(4471), CrashRelay: treasury 150,000, no row, nothing credited, total 150,000; 25 distinct states, 9 violations with `-continue`. AllSettled fails too (`OutboxAckFirstLive.cfg`, lasso ending in Stuttering with 4471 never credited). The plain Conserved (without the memory term) would fail even without a crash, right after Take, because the only durable record of the debt is gone.

Problems (hint ladders; never a solution on the page):
1. Credit first with a keyed credit settles every run. Write an invariant that captures “every finished run adds up” and check it; then argue whether it is enough for finance.
   - Hint 1: “finished” is a state predicate: every job is done.
   - Hint 2: use the same implication shape as ConservedAtRest: AllDone => Conserved.
   - Hint 3: compare with and without the key. (TLC: `CreditFirstKeyDone.cfg` no error, 39 generated, 23 distinct; `CreditFirstDone.cfg` without the key violated in 6 steps, 26 distinct found. `check.py`: both final states of credit first with key total 200,000.)
   - Hint 4: when does month-end run? Ask whether a reconciliation that lands right after the crash sees an at-rest state that is off (4 of 23 states are).
2. A relay that times out on the loan ledger's reply cannot tell whether the credit landed, so it delivers again without crashing. Change Deliver so it may run whenever the row exists, and check both credits.
   - Hint 1: drop the `l \notin held` line.
   - Hint 2: a Deliver that changes nothing is a stuttering step; it does not count as work for weak fairness.
   - Hint 3: without the key, how many steps until the total is wrong, and is the state space still finite? (TLC: `OutboxAny.cfg` keyed: no error, 82 generated, 37 distinct; `OutboxAnyNoKey.cfg`: Conserved violated in 3 steps, Pay(4471), Deliver(4471), Deliver(4471), and the credit can grow without bound, so `check.py` replays that trace rather than exploring the whole space.)
3. Repayments flow the other way: the bakery repays €5,000 into its loan account, and the treasury must receive it. Design the outbox for that direction: which database holds the outbox, what is the key, and what is the conservation invariant? Write a spec and check it with 2 loans.
   - Hint 1: the outbox lives next to the first write, in the system that records the repayment.
   - Hint 2: the treasury's credit needs a key that names the repayment, not the loan (a loan is repaid many times).
   - Hint 3: the pot is unchanged; the invariant still sums treasury, loan accounts and what is owed. (Not run; open design, no number is claimed.)
