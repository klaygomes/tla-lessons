The Payout Desk/Lesson 7/Teacher's sheet

Does the Code Match the Spec?

Teacher's sheet · Lesson text (draft)

Overview

Objective: after this, you can write a system's purpose as a small abstract spec (PayOnce, one step), write a refinement mapping from a detailed design to it with INSTANCE … WITH, check the refinement in TLC as PROPERTY Refines with Refines == Abstract!Spec, explain why stuttering steps make refinement possible, read a refinement failure (a forbidden step with its abstract column, or a lasso against the abstract spec's fairness), and explain why refinement carries liveness only when the abstract spec includes fairness and the design makes fairness promises of its own.

Source: none on file. Original course written from general knowledge of TLA+ (Lamport's TLA+, refinement mappings, the TLC model checker). Every number below comes from a real TLC run of checks/*.tla (log in checks/tlc-results.txt, regenerated by checks/tlc.sh) and is mirrored by checks/check.py, a Python breadth-first checker that translates every step through the mapping and searches stuttering lassos; every state count matches TLC.

Bug: the pilot of the queue design marks loan 4471 paid and deletes its message before calling the bank; a deploy restarts worker B between the two, the books say paid, the bank never pays. The team's own checks (NoDoublePayout, TablePaid == <>(status = "paid")) pass in all 60 states.

Fix: act 2 of the lab: with the mapping fixed (PayOnce's status means the bank has paid) and Crash on, reorder to send, confirm, mark, keep the idempotency key and a lease that is weakly fair to run out; Refines passes with 2 workers (99 states) and 3 workers (431 states).

Key words: abstract spec (not "high-level spec" after its one gloss, not "model", "contract"), design (the detailed spec; "low-level spec" only in the one gloss; not "implementation"), refinement mapping (not "abstraction function", "translation" as a term after it is named; "translate" stays the verb for applying it), refines (not "implements", "conforms to", "satisfies"), refinement, Pay step, stuttering step, PayOnce, QueueDesk, the pilot (the mark-first design), message (not "job", "task", "event"), queue, lease (not "visibility timeout"), Receive (the action; pc "claimed"), Expire, Send, Confirm (the bank's step), Mark, Crash, idempotency key, worker, loan, transfer, payout desk, behavior, step, state, invariant, lasso, weak fairness, TLC.

Terms (new in this lesson; earlier terms reused unchanged):

  • abstract spec: a smaller spec that says what a system does and leaves out how; here PayOnce, two variables, one action, two states.
  • refinement mapping: for each variable of the abstract spec, an expression over the design's variables saying what it means; here status ← PaidAtBank (paid once the bank has made a transfer), transfers ← transfers.
  • refines: a design refines an abstract spec when every behavior of the design, translated state by state through the mapping, is a behavior the abstract spec allows (first state satisfies its Init, every step is one of its steps or a stuttering step, its fairness holds). In TLA+: Spec => Abstract!Spec.
  • INSTANCE … WITH (notation): brings in a module with each of its variables replaced by an expression; A!F names definition F of instance A. Same-named variables are substituted implicitly.
  • (reused, given a new role) stuttering step: a step of the design that leaves the mapped abstract variables unchanged is a stuttering step of the abstract spec.

Misconception: "the checker passed my properties, so the design does what it should." Why wrong: the pilot passed NoDoublePayout and TablePaid in all 60 states and the money never moved; properties are the ones you thought of, refinement brings every property of the abstract spec.

Second misconception: "the mapping is a detail; map status to status." Why wrong: the identity mapping fails a correct design (step 3, Confirm) and the Liar mapping (transfers ← table says paid) makes the broken pilot pass in 60 states; the mapping is half of the claim.

Third misconception: "refinement is a safety check." Why wrong: the pilot and the no-lease-fairness design pass the step half (RefinesSafety) and fail Refines only through PayOnce's WF_avars(Pay); drop that WF and they pass. "What people get wrong" on the page: refinement is one more invariant; the mapping is a detail (Liar, with TLA+, read-aloud and Python); the design refines PayOnce so the service is correct (the TypeScript is a further level, as in lesson 5; 2 and 3 workers only, lesson 8).

Concept check, answers (never shown to the learner):

  1. Step 7, Confirm(B): (paid, 1) → (paid, 1), a stuttering step, because the bank already holds key 4471 and transfers stays 1. Without the key it would be (paid, 1) → (paid, 2), neither Pay (status was not approved) nor stutter: a forbidden step, exactly the last step of the NoKey trace. Step 9, Mark(B): a stutter; Mark changes status, queue and pc, none of which PaidAtBank reads. Common wrong answer: "Mark(B) is a second Pay because it writes paid".
  2. Only the design without keys still fails: its failure is a forbidden step, in the step half. The pilot (-config SafetyOnly.cfg MarkFirst.tla: no error, 60 states), the design with no fairness on Expire (-config SafetyOnly.cfg NoLease.tla: no error, 99) and the good design through the always-"approved, 0" mapping (TrivialSafety.cfg: no error, 99) all pass, because their only failure was a lasso against WF_avars(Pay). With the WF, the always-approved mapping fails (Trivial.cfg, temporal properties violated; the printed lasso varied between runs, e.g. Receive(A), Send(A), Confirm(A), Expire, Receive(B), Crash(B), Mark(A), Stuttering). Point: without fairness in the abstract spec, a mapping that says nothing ever happens refines anything.
  3. Mapping: PaidAtBank == IF transfers > 0 THEN "paid" ELSE "approved", Abstract == INSTANCE PayOnce WITH status <- PaidAtBank, transfers <- transfers. It refines PayOnce, both halves: IdemKeyRefines.tla, no error, 25 generated, 23 distinct, depth 7 (the same 23 states as lesson 2). The Pay step is the first keyed Send; the resend after Resume is a stutter. Accept answers that predict the identity mapping fails because lesson 2's status takes the value "claimed", which PayOnce never has (reasoning, not run).

Problems (hint ladders; never a solution on the page):

  1. Split the write. In the code, setStatus and queue.delete are two calls. Change QueueDesk so Mark only writes paid (pc "marked") and a new Delete(w) deletes the message (pc "done"), with Crash allowed at "marked". Does it still refine PayOnce? Answer (SplitMark.tla): yes, no error, 445 generated, 171 distinct, depth 15; check.py split mark: 171 states, 24 Pay, 420 stutter.
    • Hint 1: Which of the variables that Mark and Delete change does PaidAtBank read?
    • Hint 2: Crash after Mark and before Delete. Where is the message, and what does the lease do?
    • Hint 3: Follow the redelivered message: Receive, Send, Confirm. What does the key make of that Confirm?
    • Hint 4: For the fairness half: once transfers is 1, is PayOnce's Pay still enabled?
  2. Books that lag but never lead. Finance wants an abstract spec where the books matter: money moves first, then the books record it, never the other way round. Write PayRecord with Pay (transfers 0 → 1, status unchanged), Record (transfers 1 and approved → paid), and weak fairness on both; map with INSTANCE PayRecord and no WITH (identity). Check QueueDesk and the pilot. Answer (PayRecord.tla, BooksQueue.tla, BooksPilot.tla): QueueDesk refines it (no error, 99 distinct); the pilot fails in 2 steps, Receive(A), Mark(A), "Action property line 19, col 20 to line 19, col 42 of module PayRecord is violated", 5 distinct states. check.py books(): QueueDesk 0 bad steps, pilot 16 bad steps, first Mark(A). Insight: a finer abstract spec turns the pilot's liveness failure into a 2-step safety failure, and the identity mapping now works because the abstract spec has a separate step for each real write.
    • Hint 1: In QueueDesk, which action is Pay and which is Record?
    • Hint 2: In the pilot, what are status and transfers right after Mark?
    • Hint 3: Is (approved, 0) → (paid, 0) a Pay, a Record, or a stutter?
